# Gripper fingers at their actual opening: stroke-binned finger geometry

**Status:** implemented on `feat/349-finger-stroke-geometry`; Safety-WG review, the hazard-log row
and the Isaac deploy-sim validation are owed (§7). Issue #349.

## 1. Problem (measured)

Isaac deploy-sim runs of a VLA restock policy on the bimanual OpenArm stopped on world-voxel
collisions of `openarm_left_finger_pair` / `openarm_right_finger_pair` while PhysX ground truth
said the fingers touched nothing: 0 mm penetration in 23 of 24 stops, the nearest surfaces 7-10 mm
or more away. 10 mm octomap cells did not remove the stops, so cell quantisation was not the main
term.

The main term was the finger model. `finger_pair` is the leader finger's link with the
equality-coupled follower finger folded in (`urdf_lowering.fit_collision_geometry_from_mjcf`), and
the follower was swept over the whole gripper stroke at 9 samples into **one** box + 26-DOP + exact
hull. The volume between the fingers was solid at every opening.

Measured here with `hull_overhang_upper_bound_m` (the largest distance from a point of the kernel's
hull surface to the real finger meshes at that opening — how far the kernel invents finger), at 49
openings over the stroke:

| geometry the world check used | max | mean |
|---|---|---|
| one hull, both fingers, whole stroke (before) | 84.2 mm | 66.8 mm |
| one hull per stroke bin, both fingers (the first design, §2) | 22.5-64.1 mm per bin | — |
| leader hull + the follower's bin hull (implemented) | **26.3 mm** | **21.9 mm** |

Both arms give the same numbers (to 0.2 mm). The 21-26 mm that remains is each finger's own
non-convexity (the leader finger alone: 20.2 mm hull overhang), not the stroke: at full open the
midpoint between the two finger centroids, 62 mm from each, is now **21.6 mm clear** of the
kernel's finger geometry; before, it was 22.1 mm inside it.

## 2. Design

**First design, measured and rejected.** K stroke bins of the existing `finger_pair` geometry, each
the hull of *both* fingers over a sub-range of the gripper joint. A convex hull of two separated
fingers contains the space between them, so binning removes the sweep but not the fill: per-bin
excess was 22.5 mm at the closed bin rising to 64.1 mm at the open one — the open jaw, the case the
stops come from, stayed solid.

**Implemented: per-finger split, follower binned.** `finger_pair` keeps its name, its joint and its
role (so `grasp_contact_links`, the ADR-0115 grasp-region exemption, the ACM and every consumer of
the link name are unchanged), and gets **two** collision entries instead of one, exactly as a link
cut into `--tight-link NAME=K` slabs does:

1. **Leader finger:** its own MJCF body, rigid in the link frame — one box + exact hull
   (`fit_link_with_tight_geometry`). The kernel's FK already places it at the gripper's value.
2. **Follower finger:** a box + `tight_geometry` swept over the whole stroke (33 positions), plus
   `stroke_joint` (the `role: gripper` joint) and `stroke_bins`: 8 contiguous sub-ranges of the
   joint's `position_limits`, each a `StrokeBin` whose hull is fitted to 5 positions of its range
   (edges included; the whole-stroke samples are exactly the union of the bins'). Every bin hull
   lies inside the follower box: `mesh_bin ⊆ hull_bin ⊆ DOP_bin ⊆ DOP_whole ⊆ box`.

Generated by `openral collision lower` (`urdf_lowering._fit_stroke_binned`) for any stroke-swept
link the manifest refines with `tight_geometry`. Today that is the OpenArm only: the Franka
`panda_finger_pair` carries no collision primitive at all (enclosure-test xfail, pre-existing) and no
other manifest folds an equality follower.

**Kernel selection** (`select_stroke_bins`, run after FK for every checked configuration): for each
stroke-binned box, the bins covering `[min(q_measured, q_checked), max(...)]` of its joint — every
opening the jaw passes through between the measured state and the configuration being checked. The
world-voxel check then runs the staged narrow phase once per selected bin hull and takes the
minimum distance (the distance to their union). A bin edge belongs to both neighbouring bins.

**Fail-closed cases** — the box keeps its whole-stroke hull, exactly today's behaviour for that
finger:
- no fresh measured state (`measured_state_fresh()` false), so no trustworthy `q_measured`;
- a non-finite value at either end;
- an interval reaching outside the bins' cover (e.g. a measured overshoot past the joint limit);
- any check that runs `forward_kinematics` without a selection (FK resets every box to the
  whole-stroke hull, so a selection never outlives its configuration);
- a scratch without `box_bins` sized, and every model without `stroke_bins` (bit-for-bit unchanged:
  `StrokeBins.WithoutBinsTheCheckIsIdenticalToToday` compares every cell).

Configure refuses (fail-closed, the kernel does not start) bins that are unsorted, gapped, of two
joints within one box, not grouped by box, naming a hull or box out of range, refining a box with no
whole-stroke hull, escaping their box, or indexed by a joint no collision link is driven by
(`measured_state_fresh()` only vouches for FK dofs).

**Self-collision keeps the whole-stroke hull** (the simpler sound option): self pairs involving the
fingers are few, mostly ACM-exempt (`finger_pair ↔ link5/6/7`), and their stops were not the
problem. Note the split still changes self-collision: `finger_pair` is now two hulls (leader exact,
follower whole-stroke) instead of one union hull, which is tighter and still contains both fingers
at every opening. The octomap self-filter reads the same two whole hulls.

## 3. Soundness

At any instant the kernel's finger geometry contains the real finger meshes for every gripper
value the check covers:

- The leader is rigid in its link frame and FK places it at the checked value: its exact hull
  contains it (0 vertices outside beyond the 1 nm write rounding).
- The follower at any `q` in a bin is inside that bin's hull: measured on 20 positions per bin
  (the fit used 5), the worst vertex is **0.033 mm** outside, between fitted samples — inside the
  1 mm swept-link tolerance the whole-stroke model already carried, and three orders below the
  20 mm world-voxel margin. The whole-stroke hull holds every one of them too.
- The kernel selects every bin whose range meets `[q_measured, q_checked]`, so the union of the
  selected hulls contains the follower at every opening of the step, not only at its endpoints.
  Anything it cannot place in a bin falls back to the whole-stroke hull.

**What changes in conservatism.** Less conservative *between the fingers* when the jaw is open and
stays open for the step — that is the point — and nowhere else: a cell the real fingers occupy at
any covered opening still trips (`StrokeBins.ACellOnTheFingerSurfaceTripsInEveryBin`), and
narrowing never adds a stop (`StrokeBins.NarrowingNeverAddsAStop`). The kernel still checks
discrete configurations, as for every other link: the leader's motion between a chunk's rows is
not swept, before or after this change.

Disclosure (CLAUDE.md §1.4): the configure log names the bin count; every `safety.collision` line
and span carries `stroke_bins_narrowed` (whether the stopping configuration ran any gripper on its
bins).

## 4. Cost

Per check, a stroke-binned box is scanned once per selected bin (1 for a still or slow gripper, 2
across an edge) over the same broad-phase window, sharing the call's `kMaxStage2PerCheck` budget
(exhausting it only falls back to stage 1 + `box_box_distance`, i.e. more conservative). The leader
and follower boxes together are smaller than the old union box. Selection is a linear scan of 16
bins; allocation-free (`NoAlloc.StrokeBinSelectionAndTheBinnedVoxelCheckAreAllocationFree`).
`robots/openarm/robot.yaml` grows by ~5600 lines (8 bin hulls of 276-282 vertices per finger, each
under the 320-vertex budget).

## 5. Tests

- C++ `test_collision`: `StrokeBins.*` — a cell between the open fingers clears on the open bin and
  trips on the closed bin and with no measured state; a cell on the finger trips in every bin; the
  `[measured, commanded]` interval selects every bin it crosses, edges both, out-of-cover / NaN the
  whole hull; FK resets a selection; no bins ≡ today; narrowing never adds a stop; configure-time
  validation; allocation-free.
- C++ `test_lifecycle_kernel`: end to end through the node — measured and commanded open clears the
  cell, opening from closed stops, no `/joint_states` stops; mis-shaped arrays and a bin on a joint no
  link follows fail configure.
- Python `tests/unit/test_collision_stroke_bins.py`: dense per-bin enclosure on the real MJCF
  meshes, the leader's exact hull, the open-jaw gap, schema refusals on the real manifest, kernel
  params. `test_collision_geometry_enclosure.py` now checks a refined link as the union of its
  pieces and, at every sampled pose, the bins covering the gripper's value. `test_schemas_fuzz.py`
  fuzzes stroke-binned geometry.

## 6. ADR draft (for `OpenRAL/management/adr/`; number assigned there)

> **ADR — Stroke-binned follower-finger geometry for the world-voxel check**
>
> *Status:* proposed. *Deciders:* Safety WG. *Date:* 2026-10-11.
>
> *Context.* The OpenArm's second finger is equality-coupled to the first and folded into
> `finger_pair` swept over the whole stroke (ADR-0114, §8.3 "Follower-joint finger" of
> `collision-geometry-review.md`). The kernel therefore checked a solid jaw at every opening: the
> union hull invented up to 84 mm (mean 67 mm) of finger, and Isaac deploy-sim runs stopped on
> cells PhysX says the fingers never touched (23 of 24 stops at 0 mm penetration).
>
> *Decision.* A stroke-swept link refined with `tight_geometry` is lowered per finger: the leader as
> one rigid box + exact hull, the follower as a box + whole-stroke hull carrying
> `LinkCollisionGeometry.stroke_bins` (8 contiguous sub-ranges of the gripper joint, each its own
> exact hull). The kernel's world-voxel check uses, per checked configuration, the bins covering
> `[q_measured, q_checked]` of that gripper, and the whole-stroke hull whenever the measured state is
> stale, a value is non-finite, or the interval leaves the bins' cover. Self-collision and the
> octomap self-filter keep the whole-stroke hulls. Schema addition is backward compatible
> (`stroke_joint`, `stroke_bins` default empty).
>
> *Consequences.* Kernel finger excess falls to 26.3 mm max / 21.9 mm mean (each finger's own
> non-convexity); the open-jaw gap is 21.6 mm clear. Containment holds per bin to 0.033 mm measured
> (1 mm swept-link tolerance). Less conservative only between open fingers; never adds a stop.
> Cost: one extra narrow-phase pass across a bin edge. Manifest +5600 lines.
>
> *Alternatives rejected.* One hull per bin of both fingers (a convex hull of two fingers fills the
> gap: 22-64 mm per bin, the open bin worst). A mimic `JointKind` making the follower its own link
> with exact geometry: ~6 mm better at best (20.2 vs 26.3 mm max; the finger's own non-convexity dominates), at the cost
> of a kernel FK change, a manifest joint kind outside `joints`, and a new link name every grasp
> allowlist, exemption and consumer must learn.
